## Main result

In southern Africa, women with tertiary education had a 12 percentage point lower HIV prevalence than women with less than primary education (95% credible interval: -27 to -2). In eastern Africa, women with tertiary education had a 13 percentage point higher probability of recent HIV testing (95% credible interval: 2 to 22). Associations with relative wealth were weaker and more heterogeneous. In western Africa, the expected difference in antiretroviral therapy (ART) coverage between people with similar socio-demographic characteristics living in different districts was 14 percentage points for men (95% credible interval: 3 to 32) and 10 percentage points for women (95% credible interval: 3 to 27).
