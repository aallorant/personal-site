## Main result

The review identified 242 publications from 2013 to 2023, of which 204 assessed inequalities in a single country and 38 across more than one country. Demographic and Health Surveys (DHS) or Multiple Indicator Cluster Surveys (MICS) were the data source in 39.3% of analyses. Few studies used summary measures of health inequality, and most used the odds ratio from logistic regression. Economic status and maternal education were the most analysed dimensions of inequality, and the definitions of both dimensions and of vaccine coverage measures varied across studies.
