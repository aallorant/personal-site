my research lies at the intersection of bayesian methods, spatial statistics, small-area estimation, survey methodology, and official statistics. applied threads run through everything: how to measure health, poverty, and access to services where data are sparse, and how to build tools that ministries of health and statistical offices can actually use.

i collaborate with the world health organization, unaids, gavi, the world bank, and national programmes — most often on hiv/aids epidemiology and immunisation coverage in sub-saharan africa.

---

## hiv/aids epidemiology

i build models that combine population-based surveys, programmatic data, and routine health information systems to describe the hiv epidemic and care continuum across sub-saharan africa. recent work covers disparities in hiv burden and care engagement across 43 countries, temporal trends in antenatal hiv testing, and the scale-up of hiv self-testing across eight national programmes. i am a member of the unaids reference group on estimates, modelling, and projections.

## immunisation coverage and geographic inequalities

geographic inequalities in childhood vaccination remain large, and they are sensitive to how we measure them. i work on small-area estimation methods for immunisation coverage that draw on dhs, mics, and facility surveys, with attention to the assumptions hidden inside common indicators. some of this work has fed into wider gavi and who methodological reviews.

## official statistics and algorithm-assisted inference

i'm interested in how quantification practices and machine-learning methods are reshaping the production of official statistics. an ongoing strand looks at the uk office for national statistics, both as a methodological and an ethnographic question: what changes when an algorithm sits in the middle of an indicator?

## survey methodology and wealth measurement

a recurring question in my work is whether widely-used survey instruments measure what we think they measure. i've examined the dhs wealth index as a proxy for socio-economic status in low-resource settings, comparing it against income and consumption measures in mozambique and across north africa and the middle east, and looking at when asset-based indices and poverty measures disagree.
